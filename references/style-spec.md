# Riso style contract

Content determines artifact, hierarchy, and composition; decoration is last.
The mandatory language is limited spot inks, paper/print texture, and
misregistration or overprint. Dialects select optional vocabulary; they do not
force headers, stickers, dots, registration marks, English kickers, or slogans.

The readable hierarchy must survive hiding every decorative element. Use source
facts and quotes; omit optional roles when absent. Deck pages should form small
arguments, not title-only fragments expanded to meet a time or rhythm target.
Posters can be almost pure typography.

No finished-artwork dependency: do not read or imitate arbitrary posters,
previous decks, examples, or previews to rederive composition. Only an explicitly
provided style reference may supply color values, grain/dot density, and offset
amplitude. Record those under `calibrated_from_reference`; record source-driven
choices under `from_content`. Never inherit wording, page sequence, bilingual
labels, captions, slogans, or title/body grammar. Supplied photos enter via
duotone only. The strict package contract prohibits shipping finished artwork.

Use editable text and vector content shapes. Generated backgrounds/decor may be
raster; never rasterize the whole page. Avoid default PPT icon/template styling,
gradients, glows, drop shadows, 3D, glass effects, generic rounded cards, emoji,
and untreated photorealism. Do not add decoration solely to appear 'riso'.

Numeric calibration lives in Python, queried through the design-system CLI.
Placement rationale: `anti-clutter.md`. Artifact planning: `scenarios.md` and
`poster.md`. Fit/measurement: `geometry.md`. Craft/delivery: `checklist.md`.
