# English slide typography and dense copy

Use this at storyboard time for covers, long assertions and slides that must carry several lines of source text.

- **Headline.** Draft the assertion first. Sketch one or two balanced lines and mark where the meaning turns. Keep names, numbers with units and short phrases together. Avoid leaving an article at the end of a line or a weak word alone on the last. A one-word line can work as a deliberate reveal; explain it in the storyboard. Check the actual rag against the illustration or inset. Widen the band, move support, or shorten the assertion when the brief permits. Preserve approved copy.
- **Text or evidence?** Present a sequence as steps, a real comparison as a comparison, and quantitative proof as a chart or direct annotation. Use a list when the facts themselves are verbal or when the slide must function as a checklist. In a talk, move secondary details to speaker notes only when permitted. In a document deck, use a continuation or appendix that the reader receives.
- **One reading edge.** Start with an assertion plus one stack of roughly 3–5 parallel points. Use sentence case and a cue label plus an optional sentence of evidence. Left-align the body, wrap below the text start, and keep more space between items than between a cue and its detail. Use numbers for meaningful order or rank. Two labeled columns work for two independent groups with a clear boundary and explicit reading order. Keep a single ordered list in one reading stream. Avoid repeating the slide title in the first module.
- **Size and measure.** Aim for 20–26 pt body on the standard deck and use 18 pt as the projected body floor. Use a clear sans serif for dense text if the type system permits; a serif can stay in the display role. Start with 1–2 lines per point. If several items need three lines, remove a competing visual, group the material by claim, split the page, or request an approved edit. Use spacing and shape in addition to color to indicate grouping. Keep required information even when it calls for a dense page.
- **Paper as a container.** Use one sheet for a checklist or a tightly related set of points. Three or four substantial independent points can each own a sheet. Stagger them across two lanes with a clear left-to-right, then downward reading order. Vary cut, backing ink and edge treatment within the deck palette. Keep the title outside the material, and keep screens, shadows and print details on the perimeter. A tight slide can use one sheet or a clean page at the original type size.
- **Review.** At thumbnail size, find the headline and each entry. At full size, check the actual title breaks, continuation indents, source integrity, body sizes and shadow clearance. For a screen-reader deliverable, use native PowerPoint list paragraphs where semantics are required, then verify reading order with the Accessibility Checker. Individually editable drawn rows are not a native list.

## Authoring controls

The authored English deck title fitter measures glyph lines and ink plate clearance. It prefers one or two lines; long verbatim copy can use three or four if the title band has room. Preserve an approved line break with an explicit newline or specify an indivisible phrase with `page_directions.<id>.title_keep_phrases: ["Thursday Evening Trial"]`. Explain intentional exceptions in the storyboard.

For `takeaway-list`, set `page_directions.<id>.list_order: ordered` for steps or rank; the default is unordered. Bind every label and detail to the source. The default `list_layout: auto` stacks the cue and detail for most lists and can put them side by side within each row when several longer explanations share a wide band. Use `stack` to retain the vertical composition or `side-by-side` to prefer a cue column; a narrow band uses the stack. Read each pair left to right before the next row. The projected sizes and item counts here are local targets; adjust them for aspect ratio, viewing distance and user constraints without losing source content.

`paper_collage: auto` considers one wide takeaway list per page, skips adjacent automatic placements and caps automatic use at three per deck. Use `on` to request the panel on a narrower takeaway list or `off` to use the open page. Both modes measure the insets and can fall back to the open page to preserve copy and readable type. An empty `element_pool` suppresses automatic placement unless overridden. `meta.elements: false` suppresses the panels. Each paper layer is an editable PowerPoint shape, and its points are editable text. See [paper prompts and acceptance](deck-paper-collage.md).

`paper_layout: auto` considers staggered cards for three or four substantial unordered items on a wide band. Use `single` for a single sheet or `staggered` to request individual cards, including a numbered sequence when the order reads clearly. If the cards cannot fit, the renderer tries one sheet, then an open page before reducing body size.

## Sources

- [Microsoft PowerPoint accessibility](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/powerpoint/make-your-powerpoint-presentations-accessible-to-people-with-disabilities): left alignment, white space, contrast, accessible lists and a recommendation for 18 pt or larger type.
- [Microsoft Style Guide: Lists](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/scannable-content/lists): parallel construction, bullets for peers, numbers for sequences or priorities.
- [Michael Alley, Assertion–Evidence tutorial](https://www.assertion-evidence.org/tutorial.html): message headlines, evidence suited to the claim, and secondary detail in notes for live talks.
- [GOV.UK Design System: Lists](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/lists/): use manageable chunks and reserve numbering for relevant order. Its web dimensions are not slide dimensions.
- [Adobe InDesign: Balance Ragged Lines](https://helpx.adobe.com/nz/indesign/using/aligning-text.html): the heading rag is a composition choice. The semantic breakpoint rules above are specific to this skill.
